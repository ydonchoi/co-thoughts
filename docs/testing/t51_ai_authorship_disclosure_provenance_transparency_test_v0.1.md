# T51 — AI Authorship Disclosure / Provenance Transparency Test v0.1

Status: **PASS — AI AUTHORSHIP DISCLOSURE CONTRACT DEFINED / PROVENANCE BOUNDARIES PRESERVED**

## 1. Purpose

T51 defines and tests how every generated Research Note discloses that AI participated in its generation and structuring, while preserving an accurate distinction between:

- AI-generated content;
- AI-structured content;
- human-authored content;
- human-edited content;
- source-derived content;
- epistemic provenance.

The objective is transparency, not AI detection.

The disclosure mechanism must not attempt to make prose “look AI-generated.” It should make AI authorship and generation provenance explicitly inspectable.

## 2. Governing Principle

Every Research Note generated through the Research Note Engine must contain an explicit AI authorship disclosure.

Recommended fixed disclosure:

> **AI 작성·구조화 연구노트**
>
> 본 문서는 Research Note Engine을 통해 AI가 생성·구조화한 연구노트입니다. AI가 생성한 주장이나 해석은 별도의 Evidence/Verification 상태를 가지며, 문서 저장 또는 생성 자체가 사실성 검증을 의미하지 않습니다. 최종적인 인식론적 판단과 검토 책임은 인간에게 있습니다.

The exact wording may be revised as a project-level presentation decision, but the disclosure must remain explicit and machine-detectable.

## 3. Why Explicit Disclosure Is Required

AI authorship should not be inferred from:

- writing style;
- vocabulary;
- formatting;
- verbosity;
- model-specific phrasing;
- statistical AI-text detection.

These are unreliable indicators of authorship.

The authoritative disclosure is provenance metadata and an explicit document marker.

## 4. Three-Layer Disclosure Model

### Layer A — Visible Document Marker

Every generated note displays:

**AI 작성·구조화 연구노트**

This is a presentation-layer marker.

### Layer B — Generation Provenance

Every generated note retains a provenance chain:

**Conversation**
→ **Research Object**
→ **Claim / Evidence**
→ **Transformation**
→ **SARA**
→ **Research Note**

This indicates how the artifact was produced.

### Layer C — Structured Disclosure Metadata

The existing confession_report field is used for the structured disclosure.

Minimum information:

- AI participation: YES;
- generation/structuring system: Research Note Engine;
- human review status;
- AI-generated vs source-derived vs human-edited content where materially distinguishable;
- epistemic disclaimer;
- generation provenance reference.

No new epistemic entity is required.

## 5. Authorship Attribution Model

The system should distinguish at least:

### A1 — AI-generated

Text or analytical structure directly generated by the AI during note creation.

### A2 — AI-structured

Content whose underlying information originated from the conversation/user/source, but whose organization, classification, decomposition, or synthesis was performed by AI.

### A3 — Human-authored

Content explicitly supplied by the user/human and retained as authored content.

### A4 — Human-edited

AI-generated or AI-structured content materially modified by a human.

### A5 — Source-derived

Content representing a source, quotation, data, or authorial claim with preserved provenance.

These are authorship/provenance labels, not epistemic states.

## 6. Critical Boundary: “AI-assisted” vs “AI-authored”

If the system generated the note's prose and analytical structure, the note should be disclosed as AI-generated/AI-structured even if the user supplied the underlying ideas.

If the user subsequently rewrites substantial portions, the document should not imply that every sentence was generated by AI.

Therefore:

**AI participation ≠ AI authorship of every token.**

The disclosure concerns the artifact's generation history, while local attribution may distinguish human edits where material.

## 7. Revision / Extension / New Preservation

### REVISION

The disclosure remains attached to the revised artifact.

If the human materially edits the revised content, human-edit provenance is added.

### EXTENSION

New AI-generated sections retain AI provenance.

Human additions are separately attributable when materially distinguishable.

### NEW

The new note receives its own AI disclosure and generation provenance.

### NO_CHANGE

No new artifact is generated; existing disclosure remains unchanged.

### BLOCKED

A non-persisted candidate generated by AI must still carry the disclosure.

Persistence failure must not remove or weaken the AI disclosure.

## 8. Branch / Merge Preservation

### Branch

Each generated branch retains its provenance.

A human-authored branch must not be mislabeled as entirely AI-generated merely because the parent note was AI-generated.

### Merge

The merged artifact records:

- source branch provenance;
- AI transformation/synthesis provenance where applicable;
- human contributions where materially identifiable.

Merge does not alter epistemic state.

## 9. D9 Preservation

D9 integrated notes must disclose AI participation if the D9 artifact was AI-generated/structured.

The provenance should preserve:

**Source Notes**
→ **Source Claims/Evidence**
→ **Integration Transformation**
→ **Integrated Claims**
→ **D9 Research Note**

D9 PROMOTED does not alter authorship attribution.

PROMOTED ≠ AI verification and PROMOTED ≠ human authorship.

## 10. Content Attribution Rule

The system must not label all content as AI-generated merely because AI rendered the final document.

Examples:

- User's explicit statement → A3 Human-authored.
- Source author's proposition → A5 Source-derived.
- AI synthesis → A1 AI-generated.
- AI categorization of user-provided material → A2 AI-structured.
- Human rewrite of an AI paragraph → A4 Human-edited.

Where local attribution cannot be reliably determined, retain the higher-level artifact disclosure and mark local attribution as unresolved rather than inventing precision.

## 11. Controlled Fixture Set

### T51-01 — Fully AI-Generated Note

User provides a research question and asks for a complete note.

Expected:
- visible AI disclosure;
- confession_report populated;
- generation provenance present;
- AI-generated content marked A1 where local attribution is represented.

Result: **PASS**

### T51-02 — User-Supplied Material, AI-Structured

User supplies extensive notes; AI classifies and structures them.

Expected:
- artifact disclosure;
- user material A3;
- AI organization A2;
- no claim that AI originated the underlying ideas.

Result: **PASS**

### T51-03 — Human Revision

AI generates note; user rewrites several sections.

Expected:
- AI artifact disclosure remains;
- human-edited sections may be A4;
- system does not claim that all final wording is AI-generated.

Result: **PASS**

### T51-04 — Source-Derived Content

AI summarizes a source.

Expected:
- AI participation disclosed;
- source-derived proposition A5;
- source provenance preserved;
- source content not mislabeled as AI-originated.

Result: **PASS**

### T51-05 — Revision with New Evidence

Existing AI note receives new Evidence that changes a Claim.

Expected:
- AI provenance remains;
- Revision Event preserved;
- new analysis attribution preserved;
- source Evidence remains A5;
- epistemic state handled independently.

Result: **PASS**

### T51-06 — Branch

Two alternative analyses are generated.

Expected:
- each branch has provenance;
- branch identity preserved;
- no branch treated as authoritative solely because it was AI-generated.

Result: **PASS**

### T51-07 — Merge

Two branches are integrated by AI.

Expected:
- merge transformation recorded;
- source branch provenance preserved;
- AI synthesis A1/A2;
- human contributions preserved where known.

Result: **PASS**

### T51-08 — D9

AI integrates multiple existing notes.

Expected:
- AI disclosure;
- source-note provenance;
- integration Transformation;
- integrated Claim provenance;
- D9 state independent.

Result: **PASS**

### T51-09 — Non-Persisted Candidate

Notion target is locked.

Expected:
- generated candidate still contains AI disclosure;
- persistence = BLOCKED;
- no claim of saved status.

Result: **PASS**

### T51-10 — Ambiguous Local Attribution

Final document contains mixed material where sentence-level authorship cannot be reliably determined.

Expected:
- artifact-level AI disclosure retained;
- local attribution marked unresolved;
- no fabricated sentence-level attribution.

Result: **PASS**

## 12. Failure Conditions

### F1 — Missing disclosure

Generated note has no explicit AI authorship disclosure.

FAIL.

### F2 — Style-based disclosure

System relies on prose style rather than provenance.

FAIL.

### F3 — Attribution inflation

All content is labeled AI-generated despite known human/source provenance.

FAIL.

### F4 — Attribution erasure

Human contributions are represented as AI-generated without basis.

FAIL.

### F5 — Provenance loss after revision

Human editing removes the fact of AI generation from the artifact history.

FAIL.

### F6 — D9 provenance collapse

Integrated content loses source-note and transformation provenance.

FAIL.

### F7 — Persistence dependence

AI disclosure appears only after successful Notion persistence.

FAIL.

### F8 — Epistemic conflation

AI authorship label is interpreted as Evidence, Verification, or Claim state.

FAIL.

These are disclosure/provenance fields, not epistemic states.

**T51 = PASS — DISCLOSURE / PROVENANCE CONTRACT VALIDATED WITH CONTROLLED FIXTURES**

The system can explicitly disclose AI authorship while preserving distinctions among AI-generated, AI-structured, human-authored, human-edited, and source-derived material.

## 17. Validation Boundary

T51 is a controlled semantic/presentation test.

It does not establish:

- legal sufficiency of AI disclosure;
- institutional policy compliance;
- universal authorship standards;
- reliable automated sentence-level authorship detection.

Those require context-specific external policy or human/legal review.
